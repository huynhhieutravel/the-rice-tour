# Cu Chi Tunnels Entrance Fee & Opening Hours 2026 — Verified Prices

The current **Cu Chi Tunnels entrance fee for international visitors is 135,000 VND per person in 2026**, consisting of a 35,000 VND historical-site admission fee and a 100,000 VND service and guiding fee. The updated tariff officially took effect on **February 1, 2026**. The site's published opening hours are **7:00 AM to 5:00 PM daily**.

If you have been researching Cu Chi Tunnels online, you have almost certainly encountered conflicting figures. Older travel articles, forum posts, and outdated guidebooks still show figures such as 90,000 VND, 110,000 VND or 125,000 VND. These rates come from earlier pricing schedules and should not be confused with the current 2026 international visitor tariff.

This comprehensive guide explains the current entrance fee structure, what the 135,000 VND charge includes, whether advance booking is necessary, how Ben Dinh and Ben Duoc differ, what optional activities cost extra, and how to accurately budget for your complete visit.

## Why The Visitor Tunnels Differ From Wartime Dimensions

The tunnels open to visitors have been adapted for tourism. UNESCO's 2026 Tentative List documentation records original and conserved tunnel dimensions at both Ben Duoc and Ben Dinh that remain very narrow, illustrating why the visitor experience has been modified for modern travelers.

- **Ben Duoc Recorded Measurements:** Level 1: ~62 cm × 104 cm; Level 3: ~75 cm × 95 cm.
- **Ben Dinh Recorded Measurements:** Passages around 62–65 cm wide and 105–110 cm high.

*(Source: UNESCO World Heritage Tentative List Submission Dossier, Cultural Category, Ref. 7038, submitted July 15, 2026. Across both sectors, roughly 4 kilometers of preserved tunnel passages are protected).*

## Do You Have To Crawl Through The Tunnels?

**No. The tunnel crawl is optional.**

Most of the visitor experience takes place above ground, including the forest paths, trapdoors, defensive displays and historical structures. The tunnel crawl is only one part of the visit.

This is particularly useful for travelers with claustrophobia, taller travelers, larger-bodied travelers, older visitors, families with young children, and anyone who simply dislikes confined spaces.

Visitor tunnel sections are available in different lengths, with travelers commonly reporting options around 20, 40, 60 and up to 100 metres. The underground experience is optional, and visitors can choose to stop early rather than completing the longest section. You do not need to force yourself through the tunnel to have a worthwhile visit.

## Are The Tunnels Original?

The Cu Chi tunnel system is historically genuine, but the sections presented to visitors today have been **adapted for tourism**. Visitor passages have been widened or modified compared with the dimensions of the wartime tunnel system.

This distinction matters. The heritage is real, but the modern tourist experience is not identical to entering an untouched wartime tunnel. Descriptions such as “crawl through the original tunnels” can therefore be misleading if they imply that today's visitor passage is unchanged.

The conserved passages are extremely narrow, with UNESCO documentation recording varying dimensions across the three tunnel levels at Ben Duoc and Ben Dinh, with some sections measuring only around 62 to 65 cm wide.

## Suitability For Children & Older Travelers

### Is Cu Chi Tunnels Suitable For Children?
Yes, although the experience should be adapted to the child. Children do not have to enter the tunnels, so families can focus on the outdoor sections. The main considerations are heat, walking, confined spaces, noise around the shooting range, and the child's comfort with the historical displays. For younger children, a private itinerary can be easier because you can slow down, skip the tunnel and return whenever necessary.

### Is Cu Chi Tunnels Suitable For Older Travelers?
It can be. The site is not exclusively an underground experience. Older travelers can explore the above-ground areas and skip the tunnel crawl if necessary. The main issue is physical comfort rather than the admission process itself. For older travelers, private transportation is particularly convenient because it reduces unnecessary walking between transportation points and allows the group to control the pace.

## Shooting Range & Refreshment Costs

### Is The Shooting Range Included In The Entrance Fee?
**No.** The shooting range is an optional paid activity. It should not be assumed to be included in the basic 135,000 VND international visitor charge. Shooting-range ammunition is charged separately, and current published prices vary by source and weapon. Recent travel sources report roughly 50,000–75,000 VND per round, but this is subject to change. Confirm the current price and minimum purchase requirement at the range. You can also visit Cu Chi without participating in the shooting activity.

### Are Food And Drinks Included?
**No.** Food and drinks are separate from the historical-site admission. Bring water, particularly if visiting during the hotter part of the day. Some visitor experiences also include opportunities to learn about foods associated with life during the historical period (such as a tasting of steamed tapioca with roasted peanut salt and tea), but this should not be interpreted as a complimentary meal included in the entrance fee.
