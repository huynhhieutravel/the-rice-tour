# Cu Chi Tunnels Entrance Fee & Opening Hours 2026 — Verified Prices

The current **Cu Chi Tunnels entrance fee for international visitors is 135,000 VND per person in 2026**, consisting of a 35,000 VND historical-site admission fee and a 100,000 VND service and guiding fee. The updated tariff officially took effect on **February 1, 2026**. The site's published opening hours are **7:00 AM to 5:00 PM daily**.

If you have been researching Cu Chi Tunnels online, you have almost certainly encountered conflicting figures. Older travel articles, forum posts, and outdated guidebooks still show figures such as 90,000 VND, 110,000 VND or 125,000 VND. These rates come from earlier pricing schedules and should not be confused with the current 2026 international visitor tariff.

Below is the clear breakdown of current ticket prices, what the 135,000 VND charge actually covers, how Ben Dinh and Ben Duoc differ in atmosphere, and how to plan your morning from Saigon smoothly.

## Why The Visitor Tunnels Differ From Wartime Dimensions

The tunnel system began developing in the late 1940s, with historical records tracing its origins to 1946. Today, the passages open to visitors have been adapted for tourism. Scientific survey records and national heritage documentation record original conserved tunnel dimensions at both Ben Duoc and Ben Dinh that remain very narrow, illustrating why the visitor experience has been modified for modern travelers.

- **Ben Duoc Recorded Measurements:** Level 1: ~62 cm × 104 cm; Level 3: ~75 cm × 95 cm.
- **Ben Dinh Recorded Measurements:** Passages around 62–65 cm wide; constricted sections down to ~40 × 73 cm.

This is why “authenticity” is better understood as the historical site and preserved tunnel network rather than assuming that every passage tourists crawl through remains untouched.

### Why Were The Tunnels Built In Zigzags?
The tunnel network was not designed as a simple straight corridor. Historical architectural documentation describes winding and zigzag passages, staggered tunnel levels, and concealed connections between them. Straight segments at Ben Duoc were generally kept under 30 metres to limit blast damage and hinder straight-line pursuit.

*(Source: Special National Historical Site conservation records & Cu Chi Historical Relic Management Board documentation. Over 120 kilometers of historical tunnel network are protected throughout Cu Chi district).*

## On-Site Guiding vs. Private Tour Guides

The 100,000 VND service component included in your ticket entitles you to an on-site guide provided by the historical relic board. If you arrive independently—whether by ride-hailing or public bus—you simply request a guide at the kiosk immediately past the ticket barriers. This means you do not need to book an expensive group tour purely to have someone explain the forest traps and underground features.

Keep in mind that commentary styles vary. Many site guides are passionate local historians with family ties to the area, though English fluency can be conversational and straightforward. If you prefer deep, uninterrupted academic discussion, personal storytelling, and flexible walking pacing, booking a dedicated private guide through an inbound specialist offers a noticeably distinct experience.

## Ben Dinh vs. Ben Duoc: Pricing, Experience & Atmosphere

Ben Dinh and Ben Duoc are separate visitor areas within the wider Cu Chi historical complex. The current 2026 international tariff is presented as:

**35,000 VND admission + 100,000 VND service = 135,000 VND**  
*Current travel sources and operators report this uniform tariff applies to both sectors.*

Choose between Ben Dinh and Ben Duoc based on **experience and logistics**, not on price comparisons:

| Dimension | Ben Dinh | Ben Duoc |
|---|---|---|
| Location | Generally closer to central HCMC | Farther northwest in rural countryside |
| Typical journey | Shorter travel time (~1.5 hours) | Longer travel time (~2 to 2.5 hours) |
| Typical experience | More compact | More expansive |
| Visitor environment | Often busier, especially during group-tour arrivals | Generally quieter and more expansive |
| Historical setting | Base of Cu Chi District Party Committee | HQ of Saigon–Gia Dinh Military Zone |
| Heritage complex | Tunnel visitor area | Tunnel area plus broader memorial complex |
| Best suited to | Shorter itineraries and half-day trips | Travelers with more time and heritage interest |

Ben Dinh is often the more convenient choice for a half-day trip because of its location and compact visitor experience. Ben Duoc is farther away and offers a broader heritage setting, including the Ben Duoc memorial area and additional visitor spaces.

Neither should simply be described as the “real” site and the other as “fake.” Both are legally protected within Viet Nam's Special National Historical Site system and are part of the ongoing nomination for UNESCO World Heritage recognition, while the visitor experience differs.

## Ben Duoc Memorial Complex & Heritage Setting

Ben Duoc offers a distinctly different pace from the compact tunnel-only route. The memorial temple and landscaped grounds provide a quieter, more reflective part of the visit, contrasting with the physical tunnel experience.

Ben Duoc Memorial Temple and its nine-storey memorial tower commemorate those who died in the Cu Chi area and during the revolutionary struggle. Beyond the temple, the complex encompasses:

- Memorial sanctuary and landscaped gardens
- Nine-storey memorial observation tower
- Reconstructed wartime liberated zone displays
- Outdoor park and picnic groves
- Countryside refreshments and dining

Travelers who explore additional areas around Ben Duoc can spend considerably longer than the standard 2–3-hour tunnel visit. Additional activities or special programmes should be confirmed separately from the standard 135,000 VND international charge.

## How Long Do You Need At Cu Chi Tunnels?

Most travelers spend approximately **2 to 3 hours on site**. A standard circuit through the forest trail, weapon displays, briefing hut, and optional tunnel crawl takes about two hours. If you add a quiet walk through the Ben Duoc Memorial Temple and gardens, or spend time at the shooting range, plan for closer to three hours.

Door-to-door from central Saigon, a private half-day excursion comfortably spans **5 to 6 hours** including highway transit. Attempting to compress Cu Chi into a 3-hour total window leaves no margin for Saigon's unpredictable suburban traffic.

## How Much Walking Is There At Cu Chi Tunnels?

The standard visitor route involves roughly **600 to 700 metres of walking** in total, according to current visitor information, generally at an easy pace with stops along the way.

The walking paths are flat and shaded under the forest canopy. Because the group pauses frequently to view concealed trapdoors, bomb craters, and historical exhibits, the physical exertion is modest for travelers with average mobility. The tunnel crawl itself is only one optional segment of this circuit.

## Do You Have To Crawl Through The Tunnels?

**No. The tunnel crawl is optional.**

Most of the visitor experience takes place above ground, including the forest paths, trapdoors, defensive displays and historical structures. The tunnel crawl is only one part of the visit.

Visitor tunnel sections are available in modular segments (commonly 20m, 40m, 60m, up to 100m). Visitors can exit at various points along the route if they feel uncomfortable. You do not need to force yourself through the tunnel to have a worthwhile and complete educational visit.

## What To Wear And Bring (Including Rain & Mud)

Dress practically for a tropical outdoor environment:

- **Footwear:** Comfortable walking shoes with good grip are the safer choice, especially after rain.
- **Clothing:** Lightweight, breathable clothing that can tolerate dust. Modest clothing is recommended when visiting the Ben Duoc Memorial Temple.
- **Sun & Insect Protection:** Sunscreen, hat, and insect repellent (particularly during warmer or wetter months).
- **Spares & Toiletries:** Water bottle, tissues or wet wipes, and a spare shirt if you plan to crawl through the humid tunnels.

**Seasonal Rain & Ground Conditions:** During the wetter months (roughly May to November), rain can make forest paths muddy even when showers are brief. Lightweight rain protection and shoes with good grip are useful. You do not necessarily need to avoid Cu Chi during the rainy season, but expect less predictable ground conditions.

## Who Can Comfortably Visit Cu Chi Tunnels?

- **Families With Children:** Children can visit the site, but parents should consider heat, walking, confined spaces, noise around the shooting range, and whether the child is comfortable with the historical displays. Children do not have to enter the tunnels, so families can focus on the outdoor sections. For younger children, a private itinerary can be easier because you can control the pace and return whenever necessary.
- **Older Travelers & Mobility Considerations:** The site is not exclusively an underground experience. Older travelers can explore the above-ground areas and skip the tunnel crawl. If mobility is limited, the above-ground route is the relevant part of the visit; the tunnel crawl can simply be skipped. Private transportation is particularly convenient because it reduces walking between vehicles and ticket gates.
- **Tall Or Larger-Framed Travelers:** They may find the crawl demanding. Official architectural survey records record some constricted sections at Ben Dinh measuring approximately 40 cm wide and 73 cm high, while preserved main passages are also very narrow. Visitor sections have been adapted for tourists, but taller or larger travelers may still find duck-walking through the passages uncomfortable. You can simply remain above ground and walk along the surface trail.
- **Claustrophobia Considerations:** The site can still be visited comfortably because the underground section is optional. You can experience the forest trails, concealed entrances, trap demonstrations, and historical briefings completely in the open air.

## Optional On-Site Activities & Refreshment Costs

- **Shooting Range & Live-Fire Costs:** The shooting range is an optional paid activity and is not included in the basic 135,000 VND international visitor charge. Shooting-range ammunition is charged separately, and published prices vary by source and weapon type (recent travel sources cite roughly 50,000–75,000 VND per round, with some reporting a minimum purchase requirement of 10 rounds). Participants must be at least 18 years old and meet physical safety guidelines (children are strictly prohibited from participating in live-fire activities). Confirm current pricing directly at the range.
  - *Audible Gunfire Note:* You can completely skip the shooting range. Keep in mind that gunfire can still be audible from parts of the visitor site when the range is operating.
- **Food, Drinks & Tapioca Tasting:** Food and bottled refreshments are separate from historical-site admission. Many tours include a sampling of steamed tapioca with roasted peanut salt and warm tea (a wartime staple food), but full meals are not included in the standard admission.

## Visiting Independently: Transport & Budget Planning

Independent travel to Cu Chi is entirely possible using ride-hailing apps, public buses, or private car rentals. Keep these practical factors in mind:

- **Ride-Hailing (Grab / Xanh SM):** Convenient for the outbound journey, but vehicle availability for the return trip from rural Cu Chi is less predictable. If traveling independently, do not wait until the end of your visit to think about the return ride; check return options in advance.
- **Public Buses:** The lowest-cost option, but requiring significantly more time and transfers. Check the current route for your chosen site before departure.
- **Private Cars:** Offers direct round-trip transport where the driver waits during your visit.

## The Countryside Journey & Destination Combinations

- **Cu Chi Is More Than The Tunnels (The Countryside Journey):** The journey to Cu Chi passes through the rural landscape northwest of Ho Chi Minh City, with rubber plantations, waterways and village scenery. For travelers using private transportation, the drive can be experienced as part of the southern Vietnamese countryside rather than simply a highway commute.
- **Combining Cu Chi With Saigon City Sights:** A half-day morning trip to Cu Chi pairs comfortably with an afternoon in central Ho Chi Minh City visiting the War Remnants Museum, Reunification Palace, Chinatown, or enjoying a coffee in a historic downtown apartment cafe.
- **Combining Cu Chi With Cao Dai Great Temple (Tay Ninh):** Cu Chi + Cao Dai Temple is an established full-day combination, particularly for travelers interested in Southern Vietnam's history, religion, and countryside. Because Tay Ninh lies further northwest along Highway 22, the two destinations share natural geographic synergy.
- **Combining Cu Chi With The Mekong Delta:** Combining both in a single day is possible but creates a very long day because the two destinations lie in different directions from Ho Chi Minh City (Cu Chi is northwest, while the Mekong gateway is southwest). Travelers with more time may prefer dedicating separate days to each.
